# TRIZ — 40 Inventive Principles & 39 Parameters

## How to use the contradiction matrix

1. Express your technical contradiction as: improving **parameter A** worsens **parameter B**.
2. Map A and B to the 39 standard engineering parameters below (closest fit).
3. Look up row A × column B in the matrix → it names 3–4 of the 40 principles that historically resolved that exact contradiction.
4. Read each named principle below and brainstorm how it applies to *your* problem.

The matrix is a 39×39 lookup. Use the live table at https://www.triz40.com/aff_Principles_TRIZ.php — give it improving + worsening parameter, get the principle numbers, then read them here. If the matrix cell is empty or the problem isn't engineering, fall back to scanning all 40 principles for analogies (this is what "general" mode does).

## The 40 inventive principles

1. **Segmentation** — divide into independent parts; make sectional/modular; increase fragmentation.
2. **Taking out** — extract the disturbing part or the only needed part.
3. **Local quality** — make parts of an object/environment non-uniform; each part fits its function.
4. **Asymmetry** — replace symmetric form with asymmetric; increase asymmetry.
5. **Merging** — combine identical or adjacent objects/operations; do operations in parallel.
6. **Universality** — make an object perform multiple functions; eliminate the need for other parts.
7. **Nested doll** — place objects inside one another (telescoping, concentric).
8. **Anti-weight** — compensate weight by merging with a lifting force, or by aero/hydrodynamic forces.
9. **Preliminary anti-action** — pre-load an opposing stress/action before a harmful one occurs.
10. **Preliminary action** — perform the required change (fully or partly) before it's needed; pre-arrange.
11. **Beforehand cushioning** — prepare emergency means in advance to offset low reliability.
12. **Equipotentiality** — remove the need to raise/lower; change operating conditions so effort isn't needed.
13. **The other way round** — invert the action; make movable parts fixed and vice versa; turn it upside down.
14. **Spheroidality / curvature** — use curves instead of straight lines, rollers/balls/spirals; rotary motion.
15. **Dynamics** — let the object/process adapt for optimal performance at each stage; make rigid parts movable.
16. **Partial or excessive action** — if 100% is hard, do slightly less or slightly more to simplify.
17. **Another dimension** — move into 2D/3D; use multi-story; tilt or re-orient; use the other side.
18. **Mechanical vibration** — oscillate/vibrate; increase frequency to ultrasonic; use resonance.
19. **Periodic action** — replace continuous action with periodic/pulsed; use pauses between impulses.
20. **Continuity of useful action** — run without idle/intermittent steps; remove idle motion.
21. **Skipping / rushing through** — conduct a harmful or hazardous step at very high speed.
22. **Blessing in disguise** — turn a harmful factor into benefit; amplify a harm until it stops being harmful.
23. **Feedback** — introduce feedback; if it exists, change its magnitude/sense to improve.
24. **Intermediary** — use an intermediary carrier/process; merge one object temporarily with another.
25. **Self-service** — make the object serve and repair itself; use waste resources/energy.
26. **Copying** — use cheap simple copies instead of fragile/expensive/inconvenient originals; optical copies.
27. **Cheap short-living** — replace an expensive durable object with many cheap disposable ones.
28. **Mechanics substitution** — replace a mechanical means with sensory (optical/acoustic/smell), or with fields (electric/magnetic).
29. **Pneumatics & hydraulics** — use gas/liquid parts instead of solid (inflatable, hydrostatic, air cushion).
30. **Flexible shells & thin films** — use flexible membranes/thin film instead of 3D structures; isolate from environment.
31. **Porous materials** — make porous or add porous elements; fill pores with a useful substance.
32. **Color changes** — change color or transparency of an object/its surroundings; use colored additives to observe.
33. **Homogeneity** — make interacting objects from the same material (or same coefficient of expansion, etc.).
34. **Discarding & recovering** — discard/dissolve/evaporate spent parts; restore consumables during operation.
35. **Parameter changes** — change physical state, concentration, density, flexibility, temperature.
36. **Phase transitions** — exploit phenomena during phase change (volume, heat release/absorption).
37. **Thermal expansion** — use expansion/contraction with temperature; use multiple materials with different coefficients.
38. **Strong oxidants / enriched atmosphere** — replace common air with enriched/oxygen; use ozone, ionized oxygen.
39. **Inert atmosphere** — replace normal environment with an inert one; add neutral parts/additives.
40. **Composite materials** — replace uniform materials with composites (layered, fiber, etc.).

## Most-frequently-used principles (start here if unsure)

35 (parameter changes), 10 (preliminary action), 1 (segmentation), 28 (mechanics substitution), 2 (taking out), 15 (dynamics), 19 (periodic action), 18 (vibration), 32 (color), 13 (the other way round). These appear in the matrix far more often than the rest.
